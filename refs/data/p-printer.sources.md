# p-printer reference sources (3D printing parts and structures)

Most publisher sites (arxiv, nature, sciencedirect, sagepub, ncbi, iconbuild, bambulab CDN) are blocked by the egress proxy in this environment. The figures below come from search-engine extracts of these pages and from trade-press coverage that quotes the primary statements. Anything marked "derived" was computed here; the arithmetic is shown.

## Regolith bulk density
- Grott et al. 2021, "Thermal Conductivity of the Martian Soil at the InSight Landing Site From HP3 Active Heating Experiments", JGR Planets 126, e2021JE006861. https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2021JE006861 (also noted in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.03234). Used: bulk density of the top ~40 cm = 1211 (+149/-113) kg/m^3, measured in situ. This is the **regolith_bulk_density_kgm3** value.
- Moore, Hutton, Clow & Spitzer 1987, USGS Professional Paper 1389, "Physical properties of the surface materials at the Viking landing sites on Mars". https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp1389 . Also Moore & Jakosky 1989, Icarus: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70015447 . Used: drift ~1150-1200, crusty-to-cloddy ~1400, blocky ~1600 kg/m^3. These set the tolerance band.
- Cannon et al. 2019, MGS-1 Mars Global Simulant (based on Curiosity's Rocknest soil), Icarus. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0019103518303038 . Also https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/18/13372 . Used for a cross-check: simulant bulk density ~1.16 g/cm^3 and particle density ~2.58 g/cm^3.
- NASA NTRS 20170005414, "Chemical, mineralogical, and physical properties of martian dust and soil". https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170005414/downloads/20170005414.pdf . This is general background. I could not find a Phoenix-specific bulk density figure through the available tools.

## Construction printer (ICON Vulcan II)
- Fabbaloo, "ICON Announces Vulcan II Construction 3D Printer" (2019). https://www.fabbaloo.com/2019/03/icon-announces-vulcan-ii-construction-3d-printer . Used: ICON's statement that the printer needs up to 35 kW at peak and 16 kW nominal. This gives **printer_power_kW** = 16. Also used: the bead is 1 in tall x 2 in wide and is laid at 5-7 in/s.
- 3Dnatives, https://www.3dnatives.com/en/vulcan-ii-140320195/ . VoxelMatters, https://www.voxelmatters.com/icon-vulcan-ii-3d-printer-construction/ . Aniwaa, https://www.aniwaa.com/product/3d-printers/icon-vulcan-ii/ . FacFox, https://facfox.com/docs/kb/vulcan-ii-2-3d-printer-all-you-need-to-know . Used: 5-7 in/s (13-18 cm/s) deposition, 4-8 in/s speed range, Lavacrete Portland-cement mortar (6000 psi), and "walls of a 500 sq ft home in 24 h of print time".
- Derived **extrusion_rate_kg_per_h**: 2 in^2 x 6 in/s = 12 in^3/s = 196.6 cm^3/s = 0.708 m^3/h. Multiplied by 2300 kg/m^3 (assumed density of printable cement mortar) this is 1628 kg/h. The range is 1357-1900 kg/h at 5-7 in/s.
- Derived **energy_per_kg_kWh**: 16 kW / 1628 kg/h = 0.0098 kWh/kg. This is printer electricity only, with no binder embodied energy.

## Landing pad (derived)
- **landing_pad_10m_diam_20cm_mass_t**: V = pi x 5^2 x 0.2 = 15.708 m^3. Multiplied by 1211 kg/m^3 this is 19.02 t.
- **landing_pad_print_time_h**: by mass, 19.02 t / 1628 kg/h = 11.7 h. By volume, 15.708 / 0.708 m^3/h = 22.2 h. The reference value is the midpoint, 17 h, with a 35% band.
- Context: Metzger & Autry 2023, "The Cost of Lunar Landing Pads with a Trade Study of Construction Methods", New Space 11:94. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00378 , https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023NewSp..11...94M/abstract . Used qualitatively: microwave sintering is favoured for the hot inner zone and polymer infusion for the outer zone. The reference pad has an inner radius of 12 m and is 7.62 cm thick. I could not reach the full text, so no numbers were taken from it.

## Sintering energy
- Ginés-Palomares, Fateri, Kalhöfer, Schubert, Meyer, Kolsch, Brandić Lipińska, Davenport, Imhof, Waclavicek, Sperl, Makaya & Günster 2023, "Laser melting manufacturing of large elements of lunar regolith simulant for paving on the Moon", Sci. Rep. 13:15593. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-42008-1 . Coverage: https://scitechdaily.com/paving-the-moon-a-new-laser-approach-to-creating-lunar-roads-and-landing-pads/ and https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1003946 . Used: a 12 kW CO2 laser with a 4.5 cm beam, on EAC-1A simulant. The paper estimates a 100 m^2 pad, 2 cm thick, in 115 days. Derived: 12 kW x 2760 h = 33.1 MWh, spread over 4-5 t, gives 6.6-8.3 kWh/kg. This is the **sintering_energy_per_kg_kWh** value of 7.0.
- Whittington & Parsapoor 2022, "Lower Cost Lunar Bricks: Energetics of Melting and Sintering Lunar Regolith Simulants", New Space 10:193. https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022NewSp..10..193W/abstract . Used: 1775 J/g to fully melt JSC-1A, which is 0.49 kWh/kg. Heating to 1000 C for sintering takes about 810 J/g, which is 0.225 kWh/kg. These are thermodynamic floors that assume 100% efficiency.
- "Sintering of lunar regolith simulants using a domestic microwave: A rapid and energy-efficient strategy for construction material fabrication", J. Eur. Ceram. Soc. 2024/25. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0955221924009208 . Also https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385498989 . Used: 11-14 min sintering runs that consumed 69 and 98 MJ/kg, which is 19-27 kWh/kg of measured bench-scale wall-plug energy. This is the upper end.
- Meurisse et al. 2018, "Solar 3D printing of lunar regolith", Acta Astronautica 152:800. https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018AcAau.152..800M/abstract . Used qualitatively: solar or xenon layer-by-layer sintering works but gives less than 5 MPa strength. No energy number was available.

## Mars habitat printing (context)
- NASA 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge Phase 3 final (Apr 29 - May 4 2019, Bradley Univ.). https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/marshall/latest-updates-from-nasa-on-3d-printed-habitat-competition/ . IEEE Spectrum: https://spectrum.ieee.org/3d-printers-could-build-future-homes-on-mars . CompositesWorld: https://www.compositesworld.com/news/basalt-fiber-composite-design-wins-nasa-3d-printed-habitat-challenge . Used qualitatively: AI SpaceFactory's MARSHA was a 1:3-scale prototype about 15 ft tall. It was printed from a basalt-fiber and PLA composite in a 30-hour, largely autonomous print and won $500k. No published mass was found, so no kg/h value was taken from it.
- ICON Project Olympus / LVMT (laser vitreous multi-material transformation) melts regolith at up to about 1900 C, under a NASA contract worth $57.2M. https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/icon-awarded-new-57-2m-nasa-contract-to-develop-system-than-can-3d-print-lunar-structures-218125/ . No rate or power figures have been published, so nothing numeric was used.
- Sulfur concrete: no source was reachable in this session, and no value was taken.

## Polymer (FDM) parts
- Bambu Lab X1-Carbon technical specification. https://public-cdn.bambulab.com/store/bambulab-X1-carbon-tech-specs.pdf . Used: 32 mm^3/s maximum hotend flow and 500 mm/s toolhead speed.
- Bambu Lab community forum, "Max Volumetric Flow". https://forum.bambulab.com/t/max-volumetric-flow/6929 . Also https://theindustrialmaker.com/machines/fdm-3d-printers/bambu-x1-carbon-x1e-settings-production . Used: the default PLA profile limit is 12 mm^3/s, and 18-20 mm^3/s is achievable in practice.
- Derived **polymer_part_rate_g_per_h**: 12 mm^3/s x 1.24 g/cm^3 (PLA) x 3600 = 54 g/h. The range is 94 g/h at 21 mm^3/s and 143 g/h at the 32 mm^3/s ceiling.
